\section{\sc Job Market\\ Paper}
\paper{Trade Chokepoints}{2026}{}
% abstract:chokepoints
This paper models the disruption of a maritime chokepoint as a strategic choice rather than an exogenous shock. In an $(N+1)$-country Armington economy, a disruptor that raises trade costs between other countries improves its own terms of trade even absent any geopolitical motive, and the gain scales quadratically in the size of the disrupted corridor, rationalizing why disruptors target arteries rather than individual routes. I apply the model to the 2023--24 Red Sea crisis using a continuous, geography-determined measure of each country pair's exposure to closure of the Bab el-Mandeb, built by rerouting 39 million city-pair freight paths over a global road and maritime network. PPML gravity estimates show that trade fell on exposed dyads, with the response concentrated among Western-aligned pairs; exact hat algebra delivers the general-equilibrium welfare incidence, with losses increasing in exposure and Iran's own gain negligible. Inverting the disruptor's first-order condition shows that each dollar Iran gains at the margin costs Western economies roughly \$200 to \$1,200, and that self-interest alone rationalizes the campaign only if it cost Iran less than \$42 million per year, well below independent estimates of Iranian support to the Houthis: a positive weight on harming adversaries is required to explain the observed disruption.
% /abstract:chokepoints

\section{\sc Working\\ Papers}
\paper{Perceived Protection: Tariff Spillovers to Product Substitutes in the Used Car Market}{2026}{with Sebastien Bradley, Mian Dai, and Yoto V. Yotov \quad {\em CGPA Working Paper 2026-16}}
Studies the spillover effects of the 2025 ``Liberation Day'' automotive tariffs on the untaxed U.S. used-car market. Using over eight million weekly web-scraped listings and a VIN-based crosswalk identifying each vehicle's country of assembly, documents an immediate and persistent 0.2--0.6\% increase in the prices of foreign-brand used vehicles relative to domestic-brand vehicles, concentrated among the newest model years---the closest substitutes for new cars---and consistent with demand-side substitution. Foreign-brand vehicles assembled in the U.S. appreciate as much as those assembled abroad, suggesting that the perceived foreignness of a vehicle is largely independent of its true production location. Neglecting tariff pass-through to untaxed substitutes understates overall consumer incidence.

\paper{Logistic Distance}{2025--2026}{with Peter Herman, Serge Shikher, and Yoto V. Yotov}
% abstract:logistic-distance
This paper employs geospatial network data to construct a novel transportation-network-informed distance variable that integrates realistic city-to-city freight routing into the Mayer and Zignago (2011) population-weighted distance formula, rendering bilateral distances that are meaningfully responsive to infrastructure shocks. Using the 2023 Red Sea crisis as a natural experiment, we estimate a saturated gravity model in an event-study framework with importer--exporter--time and country-pair fixed effects on monthly bilateral trade data. Treated country pairs are those whose network-informed distance rises meaningfully following the closure of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, identified against a control group of unaffected pairs.
% /abstract:logistic-distance

\paper{Dynamic Distance, Trade, and Welfare}{2025--2026}{with James E. Anderson, Peter Herman, Serge Shikher, and Yoto V. Yotov}
% abstract:dynamic-distance
We introduce a time-varying measure of distance between and within countries and demonstrate its usefulness in international economics applications. Built from annual population data for 6,162 cities in 235 countries from 1950 to 2024, the measure improves gravity estimation by enabling theoretically consistent distance estimates in the presence of bilateral fixed effects, mitigating endogeneity concerns related to unobservable factors. We confirm the previous finding that the effects of distance have fallen over time and show that this decline has been driven primarily by affluent countries. Within a general equilibrium setting, we demonstrate that a counterfactual reversion to mid-century distances produces large welfare changes ranging from $-$1.4\% to +2.4\%, suggesting that geographic population shifts since the mid-twentieth century have benefited some countries and hurt others. U.S. migration to the South and West has had particularly large impacts on trade and welfare in Canada and Mexico.
% /abstract:dynamic-distance

\section{\sc Work in\\ Progress}
\paper{Shrouded by Statute: Mandated Used-Car Warranties and the Market}{2026}{}
Asks whether state used-car lemon laws, which often require dealers to attach a short express warranty to vehicles sold below sharp odometer cutoffs (e.g., 125,000 miles in Massachusetts), generate price premiums. Using 9.9 million web-scraped listings and a regression-discontinuity design with rich fixed effects, finds no warranty premium, alongside pronounced bunching of odometer readings just above the Massachusetts cutoff and reallocation of near-cutoff inventory across state lines. Rationalizes both facts with a shrouded-attribute model in the spirit of Gabaix and Laibson (2006): when buyers unaware of the mandate are the marginal buyers of covered vehicles, the equilibrium premium is zero regardless of the dealer's cost; dealers have no private incentive to debias buyers because disclosure raises claims costs, cannibalizes service-contract sales, and is a public good among rivals; and the resulting unpriced cost acts as a tax on covered sales that is shifted onto the quantity margin. Calibrated simulations reproduce the observed magnitudes of both the null price effect and the Massachusetts bunching.

\paper{EQUIP: Enterprise for Quantitative International Policy}{2025--present}{Center for Global Policy Analysis, Drexel University}
A large-scale computable general-equilibrium policy-evaluation platform under development at Drexel's Center for Global Policy Analysis. EQUIP embeds highly granular input--output linkages across industries and countries within a structural gravity framework and is built on state-of-the-art trade and production data, enabling ex ante quantification of how trade-policy shocks propagate through global value chains to sector-level output, trade flows, and welfare.

\paper{Arteries: A Multimodal Network Graph of U.S. Domestic Freight}{2026}{Python package}
A Python package that fuses highly granular OpenStreetMap geometries for roads, freight rail, ports, and inland waterways into a single routable multimodal graph of internal U.S. goods movement. Intermodal transfer nodes link the modes into one network, supporting route-level distance measurement, freight-flow assignment, and chokepoint and resilience analysis at fine spatial resolution.

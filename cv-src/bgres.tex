\section{\sc Working Papers}
{\em ``Transportation Choke Points, Trade, and Logistic Distance''} \hfill {\bf Job Market Paper, 2026}\\
Constructs a novel transportation-network-informed distance variable that incorporates realistic city-to-city routing into the Mayer and Zignago (2011) population-weighted distance formula, rendering bilateral distances responsive to infrastructure shocks. Applies the measure to the 2023 Red Sea crisis as a natural experiment, estimating a saturated gravity model in an event-study framework on monthly bilateral trade data.
\vspace{.08in}

{\em ``Automotive Tariffs, Spillover Effects, and the Used Car Market''} \hfill {\bf 2025--2026}\\
Constructs a dynamically web-scraped dataset of approximately nine million U.S. used vehicle listings (April--October 2025) to study how automotive import tariffs propagate into the secondary vehicle market, using an event-study design around the 2025 tariff announcement and implementation dates and comparing price dynamics by country of manufacture and by brand origin.
\vspace{.08in}

{\em ``Time-Varying Distance and Trade''} \hfill {\bf 2025--2026}\\
Extends the Mayer and Zignago (2011) population-weighted distance formula to allow bilateral distances to fluctuate over time using annual city-level population data from the mid-twentieth century onward, and analyzes within a gravity framework how long-run shifts in the spatial distribution of population have shaped bilateral trade patterns.
\vspace{.08in}

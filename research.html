Job Market Paper

Chokepoints JMP

Solo-authored

2026

Abstract

Why would a country deliberately disrupt a maritime chokepoint? This paper develops a structural model of strategic maritime chokepoint disruption in an (N+1)-country Armington trade economy. An agitator raises trade costs on a corridor of rival links while leaving its own routes untouched, generating terms-of-trade gains that increase with the size of the disrupted corridor. The model yields an augmented structural gravity system and an augmented ACR welfare decomposition that isolates a strategic terms-of-trade channel. I apply the model to the Red Sea crisis, constructing dyad-specific rerouting intensities from a global network of more than 6,000 cities to estimate trade elasticities using a PPML event-study framework and quantify the general-equilibrium incidence of the disruption.

Transportation Choke Points, Trade, and Logistic Distance

Joint with Peter Herman, Serge Shikher, and Yoto V. Yotov

2025–2026

Abstract

This paper constructs a novel transportation-network-informed distance variable that incorporates realistic city-to-city routing into the Mayer and Zignago (2011) population-weighted distance formula, rendering bilateral distances responsive to infrastructure shocks. Using the 2023 Red Sea crisis as a natural experiment, we estimate a saturated gravity model in an event-study framework with importer–exporter–time and country-pair fixed effects on monthly bilateral trade data. Treated country pairs are those whose network-informed distance rises meaningfully following the closure of the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait, identified against a control group of unaffected pairs.

Automotive Tariffs, Spillover Effects, and the Used Car Market

Joint with Sébastien Bradley, Mian Dai, and Yoto V. Yotov

2025–2026

Abstract

This paper constructs a dynamically web-scraped dataset of approximately nine million used vehicle listings in the United States spanning April through October 2025 to study how automotive import tariffs propagate into the secondary vehicle market. Exploiting both the Liberation Day tariff announcement and the subsequent implementation of automotive tariffs, we estimate event-study models comparing price dynamics by country of manufacture and by brand origin to measure the incidence of tariff exposure in the used car market.

Time-Varying Distance and Trade

Joint with Peter Herman, Serge Shikher, and Yoto V. Yotov

2025–2026

Abstract

This paper extends the Mayer and Zignago (2011) population-weighted distance formula to allow bilateral distances to evolve over time using annual city-level population data from the mid-twentieth century onward. Within a structural gravity framework, we examine how long-run changes in the spatial distribution of population have reshaped bilateral trade costs and international trade patterns.

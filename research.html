Job Market Paper

Trade Chokepoints JMP

2026

Abstract

Trade chokepoints have become targets of armed disruption, yet existing work treats such episodes as exogenous hazards. This paper models chokepoint disruption as a choice. In an (N+1)-country Armington economy, a disruptor may pay to raise trade costs between other countries, diverting demand toward itself. Disruption therefore improves the disruptor's terms of trade even absent any geopolitical motive, and the gain rises with the share of world trade the disrupted corridor carries. I take the model to the 2023–24 Red Sea crisis using a new, network-based measure of logistical distance that captures each country pair's exposure to disruption of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. Trade fell on exposed pairs, most sharply among Western-aligned pairs, and welfare losses rise with exposure while Iran's own gain is small. Inverting the disruptor's first-order condition reveals extraordinary leverage: at the margin, intensifying the campaign would gain Iran $48 to $78 million per year while costing the average Western economy $1.6 to $8.1 billion. Pure self-interest can rationalize the campaign only if it cost Iran under $78 million per year, below even the lowest independent estimate of Iran's annual support to the Houthis. Rationalizing it therefore requires a strictly positive weight on harming adversaries: in the benchmark calibration, Iran is willing to give up one dollar of its own welfare to destroy roughly $12 of Western welfare.

Perceived Protection: Tariff Spillovers to Product Substitutes in the Used Car Market

Joint with Sebastien Bradley, Mian Dai, and Yoto V. Yotov

2026 · CGPA Working Paper 2026-16

Abstract

We study the spillover effects of the 2025 “Liberation Day” automotive tariffs on the untaxed U.S. used-car market. Using over eight million weekly listings and a VIN-based crosswalk identifying each vehicle's country of assembly, we document an immediate and persistent 0.2–0.6% increase in the prices of foreign-brand used vehicles relative to domestic-brand vehicles. The effect is concentrated among the newest vehicles—the closest substitutes for new cars—consistent with demand-side substitution. We also find that foreign-brand vehicles produced in the U.S. appreciate as much as those assembled abroad, suggesting that the perceived foreignness of used vehicles may be largely independent of true production location. Neglecting tariff pass-through to untaxed product substitutes understates overall consumer incidence.

Logistic Distance

Joint with Peter Herman, Serge Shikher, and Yoto V. Yotov

2026

Abstract

This paper employs geospatial network data to construct a novel transportation-network-informed distance variable that integrates realistic city-to-city freight routing into the Mayer and Zignago (2011) population-weighted distance formula, rendering bilateral distances that are meaningfully responsive to infrastructure shocks. Using the 2023 Red Sea crisis as a natural experiment, we estimate a saturated gravity model in an event-study framework with importer–exporter–time and country-pair fixed effects on monthly bilateral trade data. Treated country pairs are those whose network-informed distance rises meaningfully following the closure of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, identified against a control group of unaffected pairs.

Dynamic Distance, Trade, and Welfare

Joint with James E. Anderson, Peter Herman, Serge Shikher, and Yoto V. Yotov

2026

Abstract

We introduce a time-varying measure of distance between and within countries and demonstrate its usefulness in international economics applications. Built from annual population data for 6,162 cities in 235 countries from 1950 to 2024, the measure improves gravity estimation by enabling theoretically consistent distance estimates in the presence of bilateral fixed effects, mitigating endogeneity concerns related to unobservable factors. We confirm the previous finding that the effects of distance have fallen over time and show that this decline has been driven primarily by affluent countries. Within a general equilibrium setting, we demonstrate that a counterfactual reversion to mid-century distances produces large welfare changes ranging from −1.4% to +2.4%, suggesting that geographic population shifts since the mid-twentieth century have benefited some countries and hurt others. U.S. migration to the South and West has had particularly large impacts on trade and welfare in Canada and Mexico.

Shrouded by Statute: Mandated Used-Car Warranties and the Market

2026

Abstract

Do state used-car lemon laws, which often require dealers to attach a short express warranty to vehicles sold below sharp odometer cutoffs (e.g., 125,000 miles in Massachusetts), generate price premiums? Using 9.9 million web-scraped listings and a regression-discontinuity design with rich fixed effects, I find no warranty premium, alongside pronounced bunching of odometer readings just above the Massachusetts cutoff and reallocation of near-cutoff inventory across state lines. I rationalize both facts with a shrouded-attribute model in the spirit of Gabaix and Laibson (2006): when buyers unaware of the mandate are the marginal buyers of covered vehicles, the equilibrium premium is zero regardless of the dealer's cost; dealers have no private incentive to debias buyers because disclosure raises claims costs, cannibalizes service-contract sales, and is a public good among rivals; and the resulting unpriced cost acts as a tax on covered sales that is shifted onto the quantity margin. Calibrated simulations reproduce the observed magnitudes of both the null price effect and the Massachusetts bunching.

Drivers of Economic Gains from European Integration: A Two-Stage Analysis of Directional Effects Across 50,000 Industry–Country Pairs

Joint with Lionel Fontagné, Nicoletta Scutifero, and Yoto V. Yotov

EQUIP: Enterprise for Quantitative International Policy

Joint with Ohyun Kwon, Mario Larch, Constantinos Syropoulos, and Yoto V. Yotov

Center for Global Policy Analysis, Drexel University

A large-scale computable general-equilibrium policy-evaluation platform under development at Drexel’s Center for Global Policy Analysis. EQUIP embeds highly granular input–output linkages across industries and countries within a structural gravity framework and is built on state-of-the-art trade and production data, enabling ex ante quantification of how trade-policy shocks propagate through global value chains to sector-level output, trade flows, and welfare.

Arteries: A Multimodal Network Graph of U.S. Domestic Freight

Python package

A Python package that fuses highly granular OpenStreetMap geometries for roads, freight rail, ports, and inland waterways into a single routable multimodal graph of internal U.S. goods movement. Intermodal transfer nodes link the modes into one network, supporting route-level distance measurement, freight-flow assignment, and chokepoint and resilience analysis at fine spatial resolution.
